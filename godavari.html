Godavari taste

Ask anyone in Andhra where the good food is, and sooner or later they point towards the Godavari.

A river that feeds its people

Before it meets the sea, the Godavari spreads into a wide green delta. Paddy fields run to the horizon, coconut palms line every canal, and the cooking of the coast is built on rice, lentils and seafood. Our village, Velivennu, sits in this delta.

Pickles: the pride of the house పచ్చళ్ళు

Summer here means avakaya. Raw mango is cut, mixed with mustard powder and red chilli, and left to rest in oil until the whole house smells of it. Gongura, lemon and tomato pickles follow through the year.

The Godavari districts are also where non-veg pickles became famous. Bhimavaram, in West Godavari, is known across the Telugu states for chicken, prawn and fish pickles. We make ours in the same tradition.

Sweets made by hand మిఠాయిలు

This region has given Andhra some of its best loved sweets, from the paper-thin pootharekulu of Atreyapuram to the kaja of Kakinada. In every home, festivals mean laddus: besan, rava, coconut and boondi, shaped one at a time.

Something crisp with the evening tea పిండివంటలు

Chekkalu and jantikalu are the everyday snacks of Andhra homes, made from rice flour and fried until they snap. Add mixture, kara boondi and masala peanuts, and a tin on the shelf never stays full for long.
